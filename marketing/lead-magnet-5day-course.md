## Day 2. Subject: Your attention is being trained (by someone)
Preview: Switching costs, and the one habit that helps most.

Hi {first_name},

Every time you switch between tasks, your brain pays a small cost to reload what you were doing. Lab studies on task switching have measured this for decades. Spread across a day of notifications, those small costs add up.

The good news: attention responds to training, in both directions.

Today's experiment: one task, one tab, 25 minutes, phone in another room. Notice the urge to check. You do not have to fight it; just notice it and return.

Tomorrow: what AI is actually good at, and where it fails.

## Day 3. Subject: What AI is good at (and what it is not)
Preview: A plain-language picture of how chatbots work.

Hi {first_name},

Tools like ChatGPT predict the next likely word, trained on huge amounts of text. That makes them fluent, fast and useful for drafts, summaries and ideas. It also means fluency is guaranteed and accuracy is not: they can state wrong things confidently.

A good rule: use AI to start, use your own judgement to finish.

Today's experiment: ask an AI tool a question you already know the answer to well. Check it line by line. Where was it right? Where was it confidently wrong?

Tomorrow: the brain's built-in upgrade system.

## Day 4. Subject: Your brain rewires itself around what you repeat
Preview: Neuroplasticity, without the hype.

Hi {first_name},

Neuroplasticity means the brain changes with experience throughout life. Skills you practise get easier because the connections involved strengthen. Skills you stop using get harder.

That cuts both ways. Hours of quick scrolling train quick switching. Regular deep reading trains sustained focus. You are always training something.

Today's experiment: pick one small skill you want to keep sharp (mental arithmetic, a language, recalling a route without maps) and practise it for 5 minutes.

Tomorrow: the three basics that protect all of this.

Manish
