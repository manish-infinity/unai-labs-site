r4: "No sugar, he'll go wild." The science disagrees. A review of 16 double-blind studies found sugar did not change children's behaviour or thinking (Wolraich et al., JAMA 1995). In another study, mothers told their son had sugar rated him more hyperactive, when he had a placebo (Hoover & Milich 1994). Your brain sees what it expects to see. r5: Volunteers spent three months learning to juggle. Brain scans showed more grey matter in areas that track motion. When they stopped practising, it shrank back (Draganski et al., Nature 2004). r6: Exam soon? Stop re-reading. Close the book and test yourself. In a classic study, students who tested themselves remembered 56% a week later, against 42% for those who re-read (Roediger & Karpicke 2006). Testing feels harder. That is why it works.
