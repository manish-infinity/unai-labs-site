Today's idea: memory is not storage, it is practice.

Here is the strongest finding first. When people try to recall something before they check it, they remember it far better a week later than when they simply reread it. Psychologists call this retrieval practice, and it is one of the most repeated results in learning research (Roediger and Karpicke, 2006).

You may have heard of the "Google effect": a 2011 study reported that people who expected to look information up later remembered less of it. Be careful with that one. When a large team tried to repeat that experiment in 2018, they did not get the same result. So we cannot say search makes your memory worse. What we can say is that recalling is the practice, and every time you skip it, you skip a rep.

Today's experiment: before you search for one thing today, give yourself 20 seconds to recall it first. Then check. Notice how often you knew more than you thought.
