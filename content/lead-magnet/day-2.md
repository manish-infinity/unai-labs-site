Every time you switch from one task to another, your brain spends a moment reloading the rules of the new task. Lab studies have measured this "switch cost" for decades. Each switch is small, often a fraction of a second, and you get slower and make more mistakes right after switching, especially when the tasks are complex.

One switch costs little. A day of notifications means hundreds of them.

The useful part: you decide how often you switch, and a little structure helps.

Today's experiment: one task, one tab, 25 minutes, phone in another room. Notice the urge to check. You do not have to fight it. Just notice it and come back.
