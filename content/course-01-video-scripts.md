# Course 1 — "AI Literacy for Everyone" · One-Minute Chapter Videos

**Course:** AI Literacy for Everyone (WordPress course ID 30)
**Format:** one short video per chapter — about 60 seconds — that plays at the top of the lesson. Manish on camera, either just talking to the viewer or sharing his screen to show a tool (Claude, Gemini, ChatGPT, Copilot). After the video, the learner reads the full written lesson below it.
**15 chapters → 15 videos.** Draft v1, created Sprint 57 (2026-09-26).

---

## How to use these scripts (delivery notes)

- **Talk like a person, not a textbook.** These are written the way you'd explain something to a smart friend over coffee. Read them out loud — if a line feels stiff, change it to how you'd actually say it.
- **Keep the language simple.** Short words. Short sentences. One idea at a time. If a technical word sneaks in, explain it in the same breath. The goal: a nervous beginner feels *"oh, that's it? I can do this."*
- **Pace:** ~130–150 spoken words ≈ 60 seconds. Pause after the hook. Don't rush the ending.
- **Every video ends the same way:** point them to the reading. "The full lesson's right below — let's get into it."
- **Two formats used below:** *Talking head* (just you to camera) and *Screen demo* (you narrating while showing a tool). Demos are more work but far more convincing for the "how to use AI" chapters — do those whenever you can.
- **On-screen text:** keep captions/keywords on screen for accessibility and for people watching on mute.

# MODULE 1 — What Is This Thing, Really?

### Chapter 1.1 — The AI you've seen vs. the AI that actually exists (lesson ID 35)
**Format:** Talking head
**On screen:** quick cutaways — a sci-fi robot clip, then a normal laptop with a chat window. Caption: *"It's a tool, not a robot overlord."*
**Script (~55 sec):**
> "When you hear 'AI,' what do you picture? A robot? A movie villain? A computer that thinks like a human? Here's the good news: real AI is way less scary — and way more useful — than the movies. What you'll actually use is a tool that reads and writes text. That's it. It can draft an email, explain a tricky idea, help you plan your week. It's not alive, it's not plotting anything, and you don't need a tech background to use it well. In this course, we'll cut through the hype and show you what AI really is, what it can do for *you*, and how to stay in charge. The full lesson's right below — let's start there."

Under the hood, it's predicting what word most likely comes next, one after another, based on patterns it learned from huge amounts of text. That's the big secret. It's not looking up a fact in a database — it's predicting language. Which is why it's brilliant at writing and explaining… and also why it sometimes sounds confident but gets things wrong. We'll come back to that. For now, the reading below breaks this down simply — take a look."

**Format:** Talking head

The chatbots you're hearing about today are the result of that jump. Why does this history matter to you? Because it explains both the excitement *and* the hype. ### Chapter 1.4 — What AI cannot do (lesson ID 38)
**Format:** Talking head
**On screen:** caption list appears line by line: *Doesn't truly "know" · Can be confidently wrong · No real-world judgment.*
**Script (~55 sec):**
> "We've talked about what AI can do. Now the honest part — what it *can't*. AI doesn't actually 'know' things the way you do. It predicts words, so it can sound completely sure and still be completely wrong. It doesn't understand your situation, your values, or what really matters to you. And it has no common sense about the real world unless you give it the details. None of this means AI is useless — it means *you* stay the boss. You bring the judgment; it brings the speed. Get that balance right and it's a fantastic assistant. Forget it, and you'll get burned. The reading below shows exactly where to be careful — worth two minutes."

# MODULE 2 — The Art of the Prompt (Using AI Tools With Skill)

### Chapter 2.1 — The art of the prompt (lesson ID 39)
**Format:** Screen demo
**On screen:** Type a lazy prompt ("write about marketing") → weak result. Then a clear prompt ("Write 3 short Instagram captions for a home bakery's new chocolate cake, friendly tone") → much better result. Show them side by side.
**Script (~60 sec):**
> "The number one skill with AI isn't tech — it's asking well. Watch this. I type 'write about marketing.' Look at that — vague, generic, useless. Now I'll ask properly: 'Write three short Instagram captions for a home bakery launching a chocolate cake, friendly and warm.' See the difference? Specific request, specific result. The trick is simple: tell it *who it's for, what you want, and the style.* That's it. You don't need magic words — you need to say clearly what you'd tell a helpful new intern. The clearer you are, the better it gets. In the reading below I've given you a simple recipe you can reuse for any prompt. Give it a read, then try it yourself."

# MODULE 3 — Thinking Clearly About AI (Risks & Judgment)

### Chapter 3.1 — Hallucinations, bias, and error (lesson ID 43)
**Format:** Screen demo
**On screen:** ask AI for a fact it's likely to get wrong or make up (e.g., a very specific obscure detail); show it stating something confidently; caption: *"Confident ≠ correct."*
**Script (~60 sec):**
> "Time for the most important safety lesson in this whole course. AI can make things up — and say it with total confidence. There's even a word for it: 'hallucination.' It's not lying; it's predicting words, remember, so sometimes it predicts a wrong one very smoothly. Watch — I'll ask something tricky, and notice how sure it sounds even if it's off. It can also carry bias from the text it learned from. So here's your rule for life: confident does not mean correct. Treat AI like a bright but sometimes-wrong assistant. For anything that matters — money, health, facts you'll rely on — check it. The reading below shows you how to spot these mistakes. Please don't skip this one."

---

### Chapter 3.2 — Your brain on AI (lesson ID 44)
**Format:** Talking head
**On screen:** caption: *"Use it as a tool, not a crutch."*
**Script (~55 sec):**
> "Quick honest question — have you ever used AI for something, and a minute later couldn't remember a thing about it? You're not alone. When we let a tool do all the thinking, our own thinking gets weaker — it's the same as a muscle you stop using. That's the quiet risk with AI. The fix isn't to avoid it — it's to stay involved. Read what it gives you. Question it. Try the hard bit yourself first, then let AI help. Use it to sharpen your thinking, not replace it. That's the whole idea behind UnAI Labs. The reading below explains what's happening in your brain — and how to keep yours strong. Let's get into it."

### Chapter 3.3 — The verification mindset (lesson ID 45)
**Format:** Screen demo
**On screen:** take an AI claim, then show opening a second source (a quick search) to confirm it. Caption: *"Trust, then verify."*
**Script (~55 sec):**
> "So AI can be wrong. How do you protect yourself without checking every single word? You build one simple habit: verify what matters. Here's how I think about it — if a mistake would cost me money, time, or trust, I check it. If it's low-stakes, like brainstorming ideas, I relax. Watch — AI gives me a claim, and for anything important I just glance at one other source to confirm. Ten seconds. That's the whole skill. It's not about distrust; it's about being smart. Do this and you get all the speed of AI with none of the nasty surprises. The reading below turns this into an easy checklist — go take a look."
